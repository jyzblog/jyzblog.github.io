Chapter 1: The First Night — Deny Trauma

The Unknown Third Giant

Alfred Adler stands alongside Sigmund Freud and Carl Jung as one of the three intellectual giants of modern psychiatry and psychology. Unlike Jung, who revered Freud as a father figure, Adler engaged with Freud on equal footing within the Vienna Psychoanalytic Society before breaking away to establish Individual Psychology.

Adlerian psychology is rooted deeply in classical Greek philosophy, offering a practical framework for answering how one can achieve true happiness. Alder predicted that his ideas might one day outgrow the boundaries of a single academic field and become common sense shared by all. His influence permeates landmark personal development works, including Dale Carnegie's How to Win Friends and Influence People and Stephen Covey's The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People.

Why People Can Change

The assertion that any person can change and find happiness is grounded in teleology—the study of purpose—rather than etiology, which focuses on past causation.

Etiology argues that past events dictate current conditions, leading to deterministic conclusions: if past trauma or upbringing determined the present, then everyone who suffered childhood mistreatment would inevitably become a recluse or suffer identical neuroses. Teleology turns this logic upside down, demonstrating that individuals act in accordance with present goals rather than past causes. For example, a recluse who experiences palpitations and tremors when stepping outside does not stay indoors because of past trauma; rather, the goal of remaining inside comes first, and the physical symptoms of anxiety are manufactured to achieve that goal.

Trauma Does Not Exist

Adlerian psychology definitively rejects the Freudian concept of trauma. No experience in itself is the cause of success or failure. People do not suffer from the shock of their experiences; instead, they extract from those experiences whatever meaning suits their present purposes.

While severe chi calamity or abuse undeniably exerts a strong influence on personality development, it determines nothing. Life is determined not by past occurrences, but by the subjective meaning assigned to those experiences in the present.

* Practical Application: Recognize that relying on past trauma to justify present dysfunction serves an underlying goal—such as securing parental attention, avoiding public scrutiny, or avoiding the risk of social failure.
* Warning: Relying on trauma as a blanket excuse traps individuals in a mindset where they surrender agency, reducing human existence to a deterministic machine driven by past events.

People Fabricate Anger

Anger is not an uncontrollable, explosive impulse triggered by external actions; it is a communication tool manufactured to achieve a specific goal.

When a coffee shop waiter accidentally spills coffee on a customer's finest jacket, the customer's immediate outburst of yelling is not an inevitable cause-and-effect reaction. The goal of making the waiter subt and apologize quickly comes first, and anger is summoned as a forceful mechanism to accomplish that goal.

* Example: Consider a mother quarreling fiercely with her daughter. The moment the telephone rings and she discovers the caller is her daughter's schoolteacher, her voice instantly turns polite and dignified. Upon hanging up, she instantly resumes shouting at her daughter. Anger is pulled out of a drawer when needed and put away when it is no longer useful.
* Non-Obvious Point: Uncontrolled rage is a fiction used to intimidate others into compliance without putting in the effort to communicate through logical dialogue.

How to Live Without Being Controlled by the Past

Freudian etiology leads directly to determinism and nihilism, portraying human beings as passive products of their pasts who cannot take effective steps forward. Teleology restores free will, emphasizing that while objective past facts (such as a parental divorce) cannot be altered, the subjective interpretation given to those facts in the present is entirely within individual control.

* Key Takeaway: The critical question for human growth is never "What happened in the past?" but rather "How is it resolved and interpreted in the present?"

Socrates and Adler

Neither Socrates nor Alfred Adler prioritized writing heavy books; both chose to convey their philosophical insights through direct, living dialogue in public spaces and Viennese coffee shops. Plato recorded Socrates's debates, and Adler's followers preserved his café discussions.

True psychological transformation cannot occur by passively absorbing external advice or accumulating abstract knowledge. Answers provided by others are mere stopgap measures. Genuine change occurs only when an individual engages in active dialogue and arrives at personal realizations.

Are You Okay Just As You Are?

Wishing to be reborn as someone else stems from a failure to love oneself. When someone admires another person's bright, charming personality and wishes to swap identities, the root motiveis the desire to escape who they currently are.

* Non-Obvious Point: It is impossible to be reborn as someone else. Accepting oneself does not mean assuming everything is fine "just as you are." If an individual is unhappy, remaining static is unacceptable; one must continuously move forward.
* Core Principle: The essential focus in life must shift from what one is born with (equipment) to the specific use one makes of that equipment.

Unhappiness Is Something You Choose for Yourself

Unhappiness is not an unavoidable fate inflicted by external circumstances or an unlucky star; it is a conscious, subjective choice.

This aligns with the Socratic paradox that "no one desires evil." In Greek terminology, "good" (agathon) means beneficial, while "evil" (kakon) means non-beneficial. No criminal commits a crime out of a desire for pure evil; they perceive the act as beneficial to themselves in that specific moment (such as acquiring money or revenge). Similarly, individuals who remain in a state of unhappiness do so because they judge unhappiness to be beneficial or advantageous to their present goals.

People Always Choose Not to Change

Personality and disposition are encompassed by the Adlerian concept of "lifestyle"—a person's overarching view of the world and self-concept. Lifestyle is not an innate trait assigned at birth; it is a subjective worldview chosen around the age of ten. Because lifestyle is chosen, it can be re-chosen at any moment.

Why do people consistently choose not to change their lifestyle even when they express intense dissatisfaction with their lives?

* Explanation: A familiar lifestyle, even one filled with minor complaints and discomforts, provides predictability. An individual knows howo navigate daily events using their current mindset. Selecting a new lifestyle introduces fear of the unknown, unpredicted obstacles, and acute anxiety.
* Warning: People choose unhappiness and decline to change not because they lack competence, but because they lack the courage to choose a new lifestyle and face the unknown. Adlerian psychology is fundamentally a psychology of courage.

Your Life Is Decided Here and Now

Excuses such as "If only I had X, I could succeed" are deliberate defense mechanisms designed to keep life trapped in a realm of unfulfilled possibilities.

* Example: An aspiring novelist who never finishes a manuscript blames his demanding job and lack of free time. The real, hidden goal is to preserve the comforting illusion of "I could win awards if I only had time," protecting his ego from the terrifying possibility of submitting his work, facing rejection, and discovering mediocre talent.
* Practical Application: Stop postponing life behind conditional statements. Submit the manuscript, face the verdict, and step forward into reality. Past occurrences carry zero weight in determining how one lives right now in the present moment.

Chapter 2: The Second Night — All Problems Are Interpersonal Relationship Problems

Why You Dislike Yourself

Focusing exclusively on one's own shortcomings and resolving not to like oneself is a deliberate strategy used to achieve a specific protective goal: avoiding rejection and injury in social interactions.

* Example: A young woman seeking counseling for an treme fear of blushing claimed she could not confess her feelings to a man she loved until her blushing was cured. The underlying teleological goal of her fear was to protect her self-esteem. As long as the blushing symptom persisted, she could maintain the comforting possibility of "If only I didn't blush, he would love me," avoiding the painful reality of potential rejection.
* Insight: Disliking oneself functions as a shield. By listing personal flaws and staying isolated, an individual creates a ready-made justification for why others snub them, preventing their core ego from sustaining deep wounds.

All Problems Are Interpersonal Relationship Problems

All human anxiety, distress, and psychological struggle stem directly from interpersonal relationships.

If an individual existed entirely alone in the universe, completely isolated from other human beings, all problems would cease to exist. Concepts like loneliness, low self-esteem, shame, or inadequacy require the presence or shadow of other people to exist. Loneliness is not being physically alone; it is the feeling of being excluded from surrounding people, society, and community.

Feelings of Inferiority Are Subjective Assumptions

The term "feeling of inferiority" (Minderwertigkeitsgefühl) refers to a subjective value judgment of having less worth relative to others.

* Example: Being 61 inches tall is an objective numerical fact. Interpreting that height as an inferior defect is a subjective choice born entirely out of comparison with taller individuals in social contexts. Conversely, tht same height can be interpreted as a positive trait that makes others feel relaxed and unintimidated.
* Practical Application: Objective facts cannot be altered, but subjective interpretations can be transformed entirely. Because human beings inhabit a subjective world, individuals retain absolute freedom to reframe apparent disadvantages into unique strengths.

An Inferiority Complex Is an Excuse

A healthy feeling of inferiority is a universal human trait connected to the "pursuit of superiority"—the natural impulse to impve, learn, and grow. It serves as a launchpad for healthy striving.

An inferiority complex, by contrast, is an abnormal state where an individual begins using feelings of inferiority as a blanket excuse for inaction.

* Formula: "A is the situation, therefore B cannot be done" (e.g., "I am poorly educated, therefore I cannot succeed in business").
* Non-Obvious Point: This logic represents an "apparent cause and effect." The individual uses educational background or physical appearance as an excuse to mask an underlying truth: "I do not want to succeed because I am unwilling to put in realistic effort or risk failure."

Braggarts Have Feelings of Inferiority

No human being can endure intense feelings of inferiority indefinitely. Those who lack the courage to compensate through healthy striving often fall into a superiority complex—fabricating a falseense of superiority to escape feeling inferior.

* Manifestations of Superiority Complex:
  1. Giving Authority: Wearing excessive luxury labels, boasting about relationships with powerful figures, or flaunting social status to borrow authority.
  2. Boasting of Past Achievements: Constantly recounting past glory days to compensate for present insecurity.
  3. Bragging About Misfortune: Using extreme suffering, illness, or tragic upbringing to make oneself "special." By declaring "You don't understand how I feel," the individual uses weakness to dominate and control those around them.
* Warning: Genuine confidence requires no boasting. Those who loudly trumpet their superiority are driven by deep-seated fears that without such displays, no one will accept them as they are. Weakness, when used strategically, becomes an oppressive power (exemplified by how a crying baby rules over adults).

Life Is Not a Competition

The pursuit of superiority does not mean competing against others, climbing over people, or asserting dominance. It means taking a single step forward on one's own flat playing field relative to one's ideal self.

* Core Concept: Human beings are different in age, gender, experience, and capability, but they are entirely equal. Difference must never be confused with superiority or inferiority.
* Practical Application: Withdraw from competitive mindsets that divide the world into winners and losers.

You’re the Only One Worrying About Your Appearance

Viewing life as a competition inevitably transforms other people into enemies. When an invidual is obsessed with winning, every peer's success is perceived as a personal defeat, leading to constant anxiety, paranoia, and envy.

* Insight: In reality, people are not constantly judging, observing, or plotting against others. Most people are absorbed in their own lives. Living in fear of constant judgment is an egocentric illusion. When competition is discarded, others cease to be dangerous enemies and become trusted comrades.

From Power Struggle to Revenge

When interpersonal communication turns into a heated argument where one party resorts to personal insults, the conversation has shifted from discussing facts to a power struggle.

* Mechanism: The challenger's hidden goal is to provoke anger, make the other submit, and prove their superior power.
* Stages of Conflict:
  1. Power Struggle: Attempting to force submission through provocation.
  2. Revenge: If one party loses the initial power struggle, they transition to revenge—scheming to retaliate through delinquency, self-harm, or severe behavioral issues.
* Practical Application: Never accept a challenge to a power struggle. When provoked or personally attacked, step down immediately without reacting in anger.

Admitting Fault Is Not Defeat

Anger is a tool used to force fast submission, b communication can occur far more effectively through logic and clear language.

* Warning: The conviction "I am right" easily slips into a power struggle because it implies "the other person is wrong." When winning becomes the focus, admitting a mistake is wrongly viewed as admitting total defeat.
* Key Takeaway: Admitting fault, offering an apology, and walking away from a power struggle carry no shame; they demonstrate healthy self-correction free from competitive vanity.

Overcoming the Tasks That Face You in Life

Adlerian psychology outlines clear behavioral and psychological goals:

* Behavioral Goals: 1) To be self-reliant; 2) To live in harmony with society.
* Psychological Goals: 1) The consciousness that I have the ability; 2) The consciousness that people are my comrades.

These goals are achieved by confronting the three "Life Tasks"—interpersonal challenges categorized by distance and depth:

1. Tasks of Work: Cooperating with others to accomplish tasks. Has the lowest hurdle because it is bound by clear common objectives.
2. Tasks of Friendship: Non-compulsory social ties requiring depth and personal initiative.
3. Tasks of Love: The closest, deepest relationshipsromantic partners and family).

Red String and Rigid Chains

Love relationships must be built on mutual freedom and equal dignity, entirely free from restriction, jealousy, or control.

* Contrast: Romantic love is a bond connected by a delicate red string (which can be severed if distressful), whereas parent-child relationships are bound by rigid chains that cannot be easily cut.
* Insight: A relationship where one feels constrained, mistrusted, or hyper-vigilant is not true love, regardless of passion. True love allows individuals to act completely naturally and freely without feeling inferior or superior.

Don’t Fall for the “Life-Lie”

The "life-lie" refers to fabricating pretexts and inventing flaws in others to evade life tasks and shift personal responsibility onto external factors.

* Mechanism: An individual who decides they want to exit a relationship suddenly finds their partner's  habits, snoring, or appearance unbearable. The partner's traits did not change; the individual's underlying goal changed, and they scavenged for flaws to justify backing away.
* Warning: Shaming oneself or others morally for life-lies is unhelpful. Avoiding life tasks is not an issue of moral wickedness, but simply a lack of courage.

From the Psychology of Possession to the Psychology of Practice

Freudian psychology is a "psychology of possession" focused on past causes, resulting in deterministic despair. Adlerian psychology is a "psychology of use" focused on how an individual actively utilizes whatever equipment they possess in the present. Human beings are not passive victims of past trauma; they possess the immediate agency to change their lifestyle and future.

Chapter 3: The Third Night — Discard Other Peoe’s Tasks

Deny the Desire for Recognition

Adlerian psychology unequivocally denies the need to seek recognition from others. Seeking approval from parents, bosses, or society is not a noble pursuit; it is a harmful tr that destroys individual freedom.

* Non-Obvious Point: Seeking recognition stems directly from reward-and-punishment education. In this flawed framework, people perform good actions only if praised and avoid inappropriate actions only if punished. This leads to corrupt lifestyles where individuals refuse to do what is right if no one is watching.

Do Not Live to Satisfy the Expectations of Others

Human beings do not live to fulfill the expectations of other people, and conversely, other people do not live to fulfill individual expectations.

* Jewish Proverb: "If you are not living your life for yourself, then who is going to live it for you?"
* Warning: Craving external validation forces an individual to adopt lifestyles tailored to what others demand. This discards genuine identity to live someone else's life. When others fail to meet one's personal expectations, anger or resentment is completely ungrounded.

How to Separate Tasks

The majority of interpersonal friction arises from intruding upon other people's tasks, or having one's own tasks intruded upon by others.

* Core Rule: To separate tasks, ask: "Who ultimately receives the final result brought about by the choice made?"
* Example: A child's studying is fundamentally the child's task because the long-term consequences of not studying fall on the child, not the parents. Parents who command or force a child to study are intruding on a task that is not theirs, provoking inevitable rebellion.
* Practical Application: Non-interference is not neglect. Parents should observe, ensure the child knows help is available when requested, but refrain from uninvited meddling. "You can lead a horse to water, but you can't make him drink."

Discard Other People’s Tasks

Carrying the burden of other people's tasks makes life needlessly heavy, complex, and painful.

* Example: If parents violently oppose anndividual's career choice (such as becoming a librarian instead of running the family business), how the parents manage their disappointment and anger is their task, not the child's.
* Practical Application: Identify the boundary of "From here on, that is not my task," and boldly discard other people's tasks.

How to Rid Yourself of Interpersonal Relationship Problems

Worrying about how one is viewed, judged, or liked by others comes from failing to separate tasks.

What another person thinks of your face, decisions, or personality is entirely their task. You possess zero control over their subjective judgment. Attempting to force others to like or respect you is an act of invasive intervention.

Cut the Gordian Knot

Interpersonal bonds that have become tangled in expectations and demands cannot be unravelled through conventional compromises; they must be sliced cleanly using the separation of tasks, just as Alexander the Great severed the Gordian knot with his sword.

* Insight: Relying on reward logic—"I gave you X, so you owe me Y"—corrupts relationships. True connection requires acting without expecting anything in return and maintaining appropriate distance. Distance that is too close creates smothering conflict; distance that is too far preventsport.

Desire for Recognition Makes You Unfree

Attempting to avoid being disliked by anyone requires swearing loyalty to every person encountered, making impossible promises, and living a life of continuous deception.

* Definition of Freedom: Real freedom in interpersonal relationships is being disliked by other people.
* Non-Obvious Point: Being disliked by someone is proof that you are exercising freedom, living according to your own principles, and refusing to live a lie to satisfy external expectations.

What Real Freedom Is

Impulsively following natural desires, instincts, and the urge for social approval is not freedom; it is rolling downhill like a stone governed by gravity. Real freedom is the active effort to push your tumbling self up from below, resisting natural inclinations.

* Cost of Freedom: The price of exercising freedom in human society is incurring the dislike of others. The courage to be happy directly requires the courage to be disliked.

You Hold the Cards to Interpersonal Relationships

As long as an individual seeks external recognition, the "cards" of interpersonal relationships remain in the hands of other people.

* Example: A philosopher who previously blamed his bad relationship with his father on childhood beatings realized through teleology that he had deliberately summoned those memories to avoid repairing the relationship. By changing his internal goal, he took back the relationship card himself.
* Key Takeaway: Do not change your behavior in order to manipulate or change another person. Changing oneself is your task; how the other person responds is their task. You hold all the cards to your own life.

Chapter 4: The Fourth Night — Where the Center of the World Is

Individual Psychology and Holism

"Individual Psychology" derives from the Latin individuum, meaning "indivisible." Adlerian psychology rejects dualism—the separation of mind from body, reason from emotion, or conscious from unconscious.

* Holism: The human being is an indivisible, unified whole ("I as a whole"). When an individual shouts in anger or blushes in feat is the complete entity choosing that state to achieve a goal. Attributing actions to "my emotions took over" or "my body reacted" is a life-lie designed to evade personal agency.

The Goal of Interpersonal Relationships Is a Feeling of Community

Separating tasks is merely the starting point for interpersonal relations; the ultimate goal is Community Feeling (Gemeinschaftsgefühl) or social interest.

* Scope of Community: Community feeling is not restricted to household, school, or workplae; it extends infinitely to include all humanity, nature, animals, inanimate objects, and the entire timeline from past to future across the universe.
* Definition: A deep awareness that others are comrades and that one possesses a secure place of belonging within the universal community.

Why Am I Only Interested in Myself?

Obsession with the desire for recognition is not care for others; it is extreme self-centeredness (attachment to self).

An individual who constantly worries: "How do others see me? Am I judged well? Am I liked?" is entirely focused on the "I." They view other people merely as audience members or service providers meant to satisfy their ego.

You Are Not the Center of the World

Each person is the protagonist of their own life narrative, but no one is the center of the world.

* Globe Analogy: A flat world map arbitrarily places one country in the middle (e.g., France on European maps, China on Asian maps). A globe reveals infinite potential centers; every point is central and no single point is absolute.
* Insight: Expecting the world to revolve around oneself leads to inevitable disillusionment and resentment. A sense of belonging cannot be demanded; it must be actively earned by making commitments and contributing to the community. Shift from asking "What will this person give me?" to "What can I give to this person?"

Listen to the Voice of a Larger Community

When facing conflict or mistreatment within a small, immediate community (such as a school class or corporate team), one must obey the principle: "Listen to the voice of the larger community."

* Example: If a teacher behaves as an oppressive tyrant inside a classroom, that authority exists only within the tiny "teacup" of the school. In the larger community of human society, the teacher and student are equal human beings. One can object directly or step away from the small community.
* Practical Application: Never cling to a small, toxic group out of fear. Recognized that the storm inside a small teacup disappears the moment one steps outside into the vast world.

Do Not Rebuke or Praise

All interpersonal relationships must be structured as horizontal relationships (equals who are not the same) rather than vertical relationships (hierarchical structures of superior vs. inferior).

* Warning Against Praise: Rebuking and praising are two sides of the same coin; both represent vertical manipulation. Praising someone ("Good job!") is a judgment passed down by a person of assumed ability onto a person of lesser ability. The hidden objective of praise is manipulation, not genuine gratitude.
* Insight: Inferiority complexes emerge exclusively within vertical relationships. In a horizontal relationship where all people are equal in human worth, inferiority complexes cannot take root.

The Encouragement Approach

Assistance based on horizontal relationships is called Encouragement.

* Mechanism: When an individual fails to face their life tasks, it is not due to lack of ability, but because they have lost the courage to face them. Encouragement helps the person restore lost courage without meddling, forcing, praising, or rebuking.
* Principle: Praise creates dependency on external judgment and reinforces vertical hierarchy. Encouragement respects personal agency, assisting the individual to resolve tasks by their own effort.

How to Feel You Have Value

An individual can possess courage only when they feel they have intrinsic worth.

* Adlerian Answer: A person gains a true sense of worth when they subjectively feel: "I am beneficial to the community" or "I am of use to someone."
* Non-Obvious Point: Worth is not derived from receiving praise or external approval ("good"), but from internal subjective awareness of contributing value to others.

Exist in the Present

Judging human worth strictly on the "level of acts" (what a person does or produces) leads to terrible ethical traps, implying that bedridden elderly, infants, or the disabled have no right to exist.

* Level of Being: Human value exists on the level of being (sheer existence).
* Example: A mother lying in a hospital bed after a catastrophic car accident may perform no physical acts or work, yet her mere survival and presence provide immeasurable emotional strength to her family. Her existence alone is of immense value.
* Practical Application: Do not compare people to an impossible 100-point ideal and subtract points for flaws. Start at zero and express genuine gratitude for their bare existence.

People Cannot Make Proper Use of Self

Human beings cannot maintain split lifestyles—operating horizontally with some people and vertically with others.

* Rule: If an individual maintains even a single vertical relationship in their life (suchs viewing workplace colleagues hierarchically), all their interpersonal relationships will subtly adopt a vertical orientation.
* Solution: Establishing a true horizontal relationship of equals with even one person marks a monumental lifestyle transformation that eventually transforms all social connections.

Chapter 5: The Fifth Night — To Live in Earnest in the Here and Now

Excessive Self-Consciousness Stifles the Self

Excessive self-consciousness in social settings—such as freezing up during meetings or fearing ridicule—is not modesty; it is a manifestation of lacking confidence in one's natural self.

* Mechanism: The individual puts the brakes on their own spontaneous behavior to protect their ego from public failure, retreating into an isolated, self-focused shell.

Not Self-Affirmation—Self-Acceptance

Achieving community feeling requires three structural pillars: Self-Acceptance, Confidence in Others, and Contribution to Others.

* Contrast:
  * Self-Affirmation: Making false mental suggestions ("I am strong," "I can do it" task is beyond one's capacity—lying to oneself and inviting a superiority complex.
  * Self-Acceptance: Honest acceptance of one's limited self as is (e.g., accepting a 60% baseline) and asking, "How do I move toward improvement from here?"
* Serenity Prayer: "God grant me the serenity to accept the things I cannot change; courage to change the things I can; and wisdom to know the difference." Focus exclusively on what lies within personal power to change.

The Difference Between Trust and nfidence

Interpersonal relationships must be anchored in unconditional confidence rather than conditional trust.

* Distinction:
  * Trust (Credit): Believing in someone based on set conditions, collateral, or guarantees (like bank financing).
  * Confidence: Believing in others unconditionally, without requiring collateral or proof.
* Insight: Doubt destroys relationships instantly because others sense suspicion immediately. Placing unconditional confidence in others is the price required to build deep, meaningful human connections. Whether the other person betrays that trust is their task, not yours.

The Essence of Work Is a Contribution to the Common Good

Contribution to others does not mean self-sacrifice. Sacrificing oneself for others is an over-conforming life-lie.

* True Essence: Contribution is an active mindset performed to realize one's own worth ("I am of use to someone").
* Example: Doing evening dishes for one's family while humming cheerfully—viewing family members as comrades—transforms mundane labor into a joyful subjective feeling of contribution, establishing self-worth without demanding applause.

Young People Walk Ahead of Adults

Adlerian psychology is a practical discipline for self-transformation that requires time to master—roughly half the number of years an individual has lived. Young people who adopt these principles early hold a distinct advantage, walking ahead of adults who remain trapped in vertical hierarchies and competitive anxiety.

Workaholism Is a Life-Lie

Workaholism is an anti-social life-lie stemming from a lack of "harmony of life."
hanism: A workaholic concentrates on a single slice of life (corporate work) to evade broader life tasks such as parenting, domestic duties, friendships, or personal growth. They use "I'm busy with work" as an excuse to rule over others or evade emotional responsibilities.
* Warning: Those who base their worth strictly on the level of acts (corporate status/income) suffer catastrophic emotional collapse when age, retirement, or illness strips away their ability to work.

You Can Be Happy Now

Happiness is defined directly as the feeling of contribution.

Because contribution can occur on the level of being as well as acts, and requires only a subjective realization rather than visible proof, any human being can choose to be happy instantly, right here and now.

Two Paths Traveled by Those Wanting to Be “Special Being

All human beings possess a universal pursuit of superiority. When healthy efforts to be "especially good" fail, many individuals execute a radical shift to become "especially bad."

* Pursuit of Easy Superiority: Delinquency, gang activity, public disruption, or retreat into seclusion are effortless ways to stand out. The individual avoids realistic effort while successfully securing massive social attention (even if through rebuke).

The Courage to Be Normal

The true antidote to the pursuit of easy superiority is the courage to be normal.

* Core Concept: Normality is not weakness, mediocrity, or incompetence. Refusing to flaunt artificial superiority or engage in dramatic rebellion allows an individual to accept their real self without needing to be "special."

Life Is A Series of Moments

Life is not a continuous line stretching from birth to a summit; it is a series of independent dots—a sequence of moments called "now."

* Warning: Viewing life as a continuous mountain climb renders the majority of existence incomplete and "en route." If an individual dies before reaching the summit, their life is wrongly deemed a failure. In reality, life exists exclusively in the present moment.

Live Like You’re Dancing

Life should be lived as an energeia (actuative state) process rather than a kinesis (kinetic movement toward a fixed end point).

* Dance Analogy: In dancing, the act of dancing itself is the complete goal; there is no fixed destination. If one dances earnestly in the present moment, fulfillment is achieved at every step. Life is fully complete in every passing instant.

Shine a Light on the Here and Now

Focusing intensely on past trauma or obsessing over distant future plans casts a dim, hazy light over the present moment.

* Spotlight Metaphor: Standing on a stage under a blinding spotlight renders the past and future invisible. Living earnestly in the present moment eliminates anxiety about what was or what will be.

The Greatest Life-Lie

The absolute greatest life-lie is failing to live in the here and now.

Postponing life by treating the present as a mere waiting room or preparation period for some future event robs an individual of their irreplaceable existence. Past and future are non-existent fictions; only "now" is real.

Give Meaning to Seemingly Meaningless Life

Life in general possesses no inherent objective meaning. Horrendous tragedies, wars, and natural disasters occur without grand cosmic purpose.

* Ultimate Freedom: Because life has no inherent meaning, the individual retains total freedom to assign their own meaning to it.
* The Guiding Star: When an individual risks getting lost while navigating freedom, Adlerian psychology offers a bright North Star to steer by: "I contribute to others." As long as one moves toward this guiding star, freedom carries no danger, and life becomes a joyous, fulfilling dance.
