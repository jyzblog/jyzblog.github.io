Notes - The Secret History of the World

Mark Booth (Jonathan Black) | August 2, 2026

Chapter 1: In The Beginning God Peers at His Reflection • The Looking-Glass Universe

The Illusion of Time and Matter

Time is merely a measure of the changing positions of objects in space, meaning that in the beginning, when no objects existed, there was no time. Consequently, the biblical account of a seven-day creation cannot be understood as literal twenty-four-hour periods since the sun and earth did not yet exist to measure them. Before the appearance of anything physical, a primal mental event occurred: God reflected upon Himself, imagining free, creative beings capable of love and thought. The transition from no-matter to matter is explained as an impulse "squeezing" out of the dimension of the mind of God into this one.

The Mind-Before-Matter Universe

Everything in this history is the "other way round" compared to the modern scientific consensus. While materialists believe matter created mind, the secret tradition teaches that matter emerged from the mind of God to provide conditions where human minds could exist. This universe is anthropocentric, with every particle designed to nurture human consciousness and sympathy. Physical objects respond to human inner desires and fears; any "jetsam" within grasp can be considered a precipitate of desire.

Deeper Laws and Practical Applications

Deeper laws of cause and effect weave through individual lives and the patterns of history, often appearing as "meaningful coincidences". The laws of probability only remain invariable under laboratory conditions where human subjectivity is excluded; in real-life situations involving human happiness, these laws are bent by the power of the mind. This "psycho-chemistry" suggests that humans can move matter simply by the way they look at it. High-level individuals are said to have "dominion over the spirits," allowing them to see into the heart of things and affect the physical state of matter.

The Matrix and Thought-Beings

Creation happened through a series of thought-emanations from the cosmic mind, which became increasingly dense, moving from ethereal energy to gas, liquid, and finally solids. These emanations are personified, intelligent "Thought-Beings". At the lowest level, these beings interweave so tightly they create the appearance of solid matter, forming a net of light and color known as the Matrix. Some beings behind this veil of illusion display moral ambivalence and were experienced by ancients as gods, spirits, or demons.

The Miracle of Consciousness

The subjective structure of human consciousness requires an extraordinary series of balances—such as memory being strong enough to maintain a sense of self but weak enough to allow movement into the future. Without these fine balances, free thought and free will would be impossible. Science fails to address the "why" of existence, which is a question of intention rather than cause-and-effect "how".

Chapter 2: A Short Walk in the Ancient Woods

The Primacy of Ideas

Plato described ideas as "the things that really are," and ancients took the objects of the mind’s eye as the only eternal realities. People did not choose a mind-before-matter universe based on logic, but because they experienced the world that way. Ancient artists were less interested in physical accuracy than in portraying the gods of the mind’s eye in vivid, bejewelled detail.

The Nature of Living Thought

Thoughts are not just words; they carry word associations, memories, feelings, and impulses of will. A thought is a living, mutating thing that ceases to be true the moment it is pinned down by language. For ancients, the world was entirely alive; every rustle of a tree or flight of a bee was a reward, warning, or premonition. Nothing inside the human body exists without a correspondence in nature—for example, intestines correspond to worms and lungs to birds.

Collective Consciousness and Inspiration

Ancients did not feel they originated their thoughts but that "Thoughts peopled" them. They experienced moments of illumination as something impinging from the outside, often as a "collective hallucination" visible to others in the same spiritual state. Great spirits could think through many people simultaneously, which is why students felt closer through shared thoughts than physical proximity.

Astro-Biology and Centers of Power

Ancient biology was "astrobiology," where the sun coaxed plants upward while the moon flattened and widened them. The movements of stars and planets trace patterns in the human body, such as the loop of the ribs. Planetary movements affect the chemical behavior of metal salts: copper salts (traditionally associated with Venus) are affected by the movement of the planet Venus. Humans possess multiple centers of consciousness: a "heart-consciousness" from the sun and a "kidney-consciousness" from Venus.

Chapter 3: The Garden of Eden

The First Reversal: Enter Saturn

In the beginning, matter precipitated out of the void as a vast cosmic mist known as the "Mother of All Living". This potential for life was nearly extinguished by a second emanation: a searing dry wind represented by the Dark Lord, Saturn. Saturn introduced the principle of limitation, allowing individual objects to exist with unique identities in specific spaces, but he also brought destruction and death. Saturn "eats his own children," and because of his influence, every crown is a "crown of thorns".

The Elohim and the Word

The word "Elohim" in Genesis is plural, meaning "the gods made heaven and earth". These gods are astronomical deities, specifically the seven spirits of the sun who work together as the "Word" or Logos. The four Cherubim—Ox, Lion, Eagle, and Angel—are the spiritual beings behind the constellations of Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, and Aquarius.

The Age of the Sun

The Sun god (Apollo, Krishna, or Aslan) rescued Mother Earth by pushing back Saturn's darkness. He "sang" the world into existence, causing matter to vibrate and coagulate into shapes. This era marked the transition from a mineral cosmos to one full of primitive plant life, described in the Vedas as the "net of Indra"—interweaving threads of light.

The Subtle Tree and Chakras

Humans still retain a "vegetable element": the sympathetic nervous system, which resembles a tree. The "flowers" on this tree are the chakras, organs of spiritual perception. The solar plexus chakra was formed during the era of the sun and connects humans to all life. The "horns" of Moses represent the two petals of the brow chakra, or Third Eye.

The Pineal Gland and Spiritual Vision

The pineal gland acts as the "sphincter which regulates the flow of thought" and an organ for perceiving higher worlds. Ancient proto-humans had bodies that were soft and waxy, warmed by the sun until they began to harden and turn pink. The statues of kings seen by Herodotus in Egypt were records of this vegetable era, where beings reproduced by parthenogenesis.

Chapter 4: Lucifer, The Light of the World

The Transition to Animal Life

In the third act of creation, the earth and sun separated, causing the earth to cool, shrink, and become liquid. This peaceful scene was invaded by a glistening, "horned" alien life-form: the serpent. The image of the serpent entwined around the tree represents the formation of the spine and central nervous system in animals.

The Torment of Desire

The transition from vegetable to animal life brought sorrow, striving, and death. Animal reproduction through sex replaced parthenogenesis, bringing hunger, dissatisfaction, and fear. Adam and Eve’s "nakedness" refers to their new conscious awareness of having physical bodies.

Lucifer and the Gift of Delusion

Lucifer (Venus) is a "necessary evil" because without him, humanity would never have evolved beyond a vegetative state. He endowed matter with a "glamour" that blinds humans to higher truths. This "Luciferic element" is infused into human physiology, causing desire and delusion to overrule spiritual knowledge. However, this chaos allows humans to be freely creative and to learn from mistakes.

The Age of the Moon

To prevent the earth from becoming a "living Hell" of desire, Jehovah (one of the Elohim) broke away to become the Moon god. He directed operations from the moon to hold Lucifer's influence in check. The first three days of the week—Saturday, Sunday, and Monday—commemorate the mineral (Saturn), vegetable (Sun), and animal (Moon) epochs of history.

Chapter 5: The Gods who Loved Women

The Nephilim and Genetic Engineering

In Genesis, the "sons of God" (planetary gods or angels) lowered themselves to have sex with women, producing "mighty men" or "men of renown". These "fallen angels" were actually the gods of Olympus who became enamoured with the material world. This era is also marked by the arrival of "fish gods" (like Matsya or Dagon), which is an ancient way of describing how animal life that would become human actually began as fish.

Precession and the Origin of Species

The secret history of evolution is encoded in the zodiac through the phenomenon of precession—where constellations move "backwards" over 2160-year cycles. Myths of a son defeating an uncle to regain a throne (like Hamlet or Oedipus) represent one precessional epoch succeeding another. Ancients understood the ordered evolution of species thousands of years before Darwin.

Degeneration and Fabulous Beasts

In the secret history, apes are not ancestors but a "degeneration" of human spirits who rushed into incarnation before anatomy was perfected. During this era, humans rubbed shoulders with giants, centaurs, unicorns, and nymphs—beings that left no fossil trace because their bodies were more ethereal and semi-transparent.

The Lantern of Osiris

While modern humans have two eyes, advanced humans of this period still possessed the "Lantern of Osiris" (a protruding Third Eye) because the skull had not yet hardened. This allowed for a "double mode of perception," seeing both the material world and the spirit world with equal vividness. Krishna’s revelation of his divine form to Arjuna in the Bhagavad Gita is the greatest historical record of this lost double vision.

Chapter 6: The Assassination of the Green King • Isis and Osiris • The Cave of the Skull • The Palladium

The Reign of Osiris

Egyptian tradition records that their most important god, Osiris, once walked among humans as a wise king and great warrior. He was known as a "Beast Master," clearing the land of monsters and invaders, and his reign was remembered as a golden age of civil and domestic bliss. His name is linguistically connected to "ourien," meaning semen, and the belt of the constellation Orion was originally understood to represent an erect penis, signaling a strong sexual current in this history.

The Conspiracy and Dismemberment

Osiris was betrayed by the enemy within: his brother Seth. Seth tricked Osiris into a chest, nailed it shut, and threw it into the Nile. Isis, the widow, searched the earth for her husband, eventually finding the chest inside a pillar in a Syrian palace—a story that survived in garbled form as the fairy tale Cinderella. Upon finding them, Seth hacked Osiris’s body into fourteen pieces and scattered them across the land. Isis retrieved all the parts except the penis, which had been eaten by fish in the Nile.

Esoteric Meaning and Consciousness

This myth functions on multiple levels: it is an astronomical record of the precession of the equinoxes where Horus deposes Seth, and a fertility myth tied to the rising of the star Sirius and the flooding of the Nile. On a deeper historical level, Osiris is equated with Dionysus. The "Cave" in which these gods are often hidden represents the human skull. The story depicts a decisive stage in evolution where the "Lantern of Osiris" (the Third Eye) withdrew into the skull to become the pineal gland, and the fleshly penis protruded as humanity gave itself over to animal sexuality.

The Birth of Death and Thought

As the skull hardened, it filtered out the overwhelming radiance of the cosmic mind, creating a private space for human reflection and free will. However, this birth of thought was also the beginning of death. Life processes like digestion are unconscious; conscious thought "steals" these growth forces, making humans comparatively sickly animals. Sleep deepened into death, and humans became entangled in the cycles of reincarnation.

Practical Applications and Warnings

The ancients understood that sex, death, and thought are intertwined. Techniques were developed to manipulate sexual and death forces to achieve higher states of consciousness, but spirituality has largely been removed from sex in the modern world. Making love is an interaction with cosmic powers that can "summon up the future". One must be warned that because we live in a "Fallen" world, myriad spirits work to destroy the fabric of reality, and true wisdom is often only reached by wrestling with these demonic dimensions.

Chapter 7: The Age of Demi-Gods and Heroes • The Ancient Ones • The Amazons • Enoch • Hercules, Theseus and Jason

The Retreat of the Gods

As matter grew denser and more inimical to spirit, the gods found it harder to intervene directly on Earth. In 13,000 BC, a decisive change occurred: the higher gods could no longer descend further than the moon. During their rare visits, they supposedly left behind mistletoe, a plant that grows naturally on the moon but cannot grow in Earth’s soil.

The Rise of Individualism

With the organs of spiritual perception enmeshed in a bony skull, humans became shut off from the gods and each other. This isolation made it possible for the first time to feel like an individual, as represented by Cain asking, "Am I my brother’s keeper?". Without this isolation, humans would be overwhelmed by the pain of others, but it also introduced the horror of humans being unable to tolerate those with different forms of consciousness.

Enoch and the Invention of Language

Enoch, known as Cadmus in Greece and Hermes Trismegistus in Egypt, was the first human the gods turned to for help. He is credited with inventing language and mathematics by naming and measuring the stars. Enoch spent years in solitary vigils to preserve a vision of the spiritual hierarchies in stone monuments and words before a coming catastrophe destroyed everything else.

The Trials of the Heroes

Heroes like Hercules and Jason represent the human spirit struggling against the weight of matter. Hercules’s twelve labors are a journey through the trials set by the spirits of the zodiac—trials every human takes unwittingly. Jason’s quest for the Golden Fleece is a manual for initiation; the Fleece represents an animal spirit purified until it shines like gold. Winning it allows an initiate to leave the body at will, communicate with angels, and transform matter.

Chapter 8: The Sphinx and the Timelock • Orpheus • Daedalus, the First Scientist • Job • Solving the Riddle of the Sphinx

Orpheus and the Power of Music

Orpheus is a transitional figure who could move stones with music but also invented numbers, paving the way for a scientific understanding of the universe. His failure to rescue Eurydice happened because he "tried to make sure," a shift away from the blind bravery of earlier heroes towards a more modern, cautious intellect.

Job and the Discovery of Unfairness

The story of Job represents the spiritual crisis at the end of the age of heroes. Job was the first human to think the thought "life is unfair," using the new tool of language to distance himself from the divine and question the purpose of his suffering. He was saved by his awareness of what he had lost—the "Lantern of Osiris" or the direct guidance of God.

The Mystery of the Sphinx

The Sphinx at Giza is a monument to "The First Time" (Zep Tepi), the moment in 11,451 BC when the four elements locked into place and matter finally became solid. Its form—combining Leo, Taurus, Scorpio, and Aquarius—represents the evolution of humanity. When Oedipus solved the riddle of the Sphinx, it represented the gods of the elements being successfully absorbed into the human body.

Practical Applications

Language and mathematics allow us to break down and manipulate experience, but they also deaden our vitality and make the world colder. The secret history suggests that matter only recently precipitated out of mind and will eventually dissolve again in nine thousand years when the sun rises in Leo to meet the Sphinx's gaze.

Chapter 9: The Neolithic Alexander the Great • Noah and the Myth of Atlantis • Tibet • Rama’s Conquest of India • The Yoga Sutras of Pantanjali

The Catastrophe of Atlantis

Atlantis was a real island paradise destroyed by a series of floods at the end of the Ice Age, around 9600 BC. This deluge is remembered globally through "The Day of the Dead" or Halloween. Legend says the unicorn became extinct because it missed the Ark, symbolizing the loss of the powers of the Third Eye as the waters of the subconscious formed.

Noah and the End of Metamorphosis

Noah, identified with Dionysus the Younger, represents the beginning of agriculture in the Neolithic era. The story of his sons seeing his nakedness refers to their curiosity about their origins—whether they were the sons of a human or an angel—at the moment the human genitals finally took their present fleshly form.

Rama and the Moral Imagination

Rama is the "Neolithic Alexander" who conquered India with an army of hominids (monkeys). His story contains the first "moral surprise": he chose to refuse his crown and his beloved Sita to help humans evolve freely. Rama is the archetype of the "Secret King" who influences history incognito rather than through force.

Shambala and Indian Spirituality

The survivors of Atlantis founded Shambala in Tibet as a spiritual fortress. From there, spirituality flowed into India, which remains a storehouse for knowledge of occult physiology. The Seven Rishis lived in flimsy shelters and saw spiritual beings working on the earth; they experienced life as a giant breath and saw matter as "maya" or illusion.

Practical Applications: Yoga

Yoga was born as a way for humans to follow the gods as they withdrew from the Earth. The Yoga Sutras of Patanjali teach that a practitioner can attain the strength of an elephant or know past lives through concentration. Advanced initiates can use a "snake" of energy (kundalini) to release an ecstatic lace-like flux of currents in the brain, allowing them to see the faces of the gods.

Chapter 10: The Way of the Wizard • Zarathustra’s Battle Against the Powers of Darkness • The Life and Death of Krishna the Shepherd • The Dawn of the Dark Age

Zarathustra and the Age of Division

In 5067 BC, the birth of Zarathustra was marked by a vision of a pyramid of light. His era, the Age of Gemini, was a time of recapitulation of the period when the dragons of Lucifer infected the earth. The forces of evil, led by Ahriman, asserted themselves, and humans began to suffer from "anomie"—the fear of destructive chaos.

The Ordeals of Initiation

Zarathustra prepared his followers for the demons of Ahriman through terrifying ordeals. Candidates were forced to swim across water, pass through fire and ice, and be cast into snake pits to prove that "he who fears death is already dead". These rituals induced out-of-body experiences, teaching the initiate that death is not the end and providing personal knowledge of how the spirit navigates the afterlife.

The After-Death Journey

Upon death, the vegetable part of nature detaches and causes the spirit to review its life. The spirit then enters Purgatory (Kama Loca), where demons tear away bestial desires. Surprisingly, in the latter part of the ascent through the heavenly spheres, the guide who lights the way is Lucifer. Without Lucifer's "necessary evil," the human spirit would be plunged into total darkness and unable to understand its ascent.

The Kali Yuga and Krishna

The death of Krishna in 3102 BC marked the beginning of the Kali Yuga, or the Dark Age. Krishna had performed miracles like feeding five thousand and healing the deformed, but he was eventually martyred, tied to a tree and shot by archers.

Practical Applications and Warnings

The "secret of thirty-three" refers to the rhythm of the vegetative realm and the number of gateways by which a spirit may travel between worlds. High-level initiates use this knowledge to slip in and out of the material realm. Beware of spiritual teaching that has no moral dimension or doesn't invite questioning; true spirituality puts intelligent love for humanity at its heart.

Chapter 11: Getting to Grips with Matter • Imhotep and the Age of the Pyramids • Gilgamesh and Enkidu • Abraham and Melchizedek

The Incarnation of Lucifer

In the third millennium BC, an event as momentous as the later incarnation of Jesus Christ occurred: Lucifer was born into a human body in China. This birth marked the true beginning of a specific kind of wisdom—rules for a happy and successful life that focused entirely on practical and prudential interests rather than moral or religious dimensions. Through this incarnation, humanity gained the ability to think about life on earth in an entirely rational way for the first time.

Rationality and Mathematics

The influence of this Luciferic "wisdom" allowed for a revolutionary shift in human thought, where numbers were no longer inextricably tied to spiritual symbolism but could be viewed purely as measures of quantity. This transition paved the way for humans to measure, calculate, and build with a new degree of precision. Imhotep, the great master builder of the Step Pyramid at Saqqara, represents this new era; he created "impossible" objects, such as giraffe-necked rock crystal vases, whose construction remains a mystery to modern engineering.

The Great Pyramid as an Incarnation Machine

Contrary to conventional timelines, esoteric tradition holds that the Great Pyramid was built in 3500 BC, long before the founding of the great Egyptian and Sumerian civilizations. It was designed as a "gigantic incarnation machine" at a time when the higher gods were finding it increasingly difficult to manifest on the earth’s surface. This structure served the great spiritual mission of the West: the "Work" of cutting, carving, and spiritualizing every particle of matter in the universe.

Shared Consciousness and the Bee

During this period, individual consciousness was experienced as a collaboration of many different centers of consciousness rather than a single interior life. These centers could be shared or moved between minds, a phenomenon symbolized by Sumerian figures with bee-hives for heads. Humans did not feel they originated their own thoughts or feelings; instead, they experienced these as the direct activity of independent spirits or gods working in and around their bodies.

Melchizedek and the Sacrifice of Vision

Melchizedek, whose secret identity was the Atlantean leader Noah, reappeared in this era to initiate Abraham. A pivotal moment in this spiritual lineage was the near-sacrifice of Isaac, which esoterically represents the sacrifice of the Third Eye or brow chakra. This mode of spiritual vision had to be "put to sleep" so that the human brain could develop as an organ for individual thinking and free will.

Chapter 12: The Descent into Darkness • Moses and the Cabala • Akhenaten and Satan • Solomon, Sheba and Hiram • King Arthur and the Crown Chakra

Collective Initiation and the Novel

Esoteric schools suggest that humanity as a whole is destined to undergo a collective ordeal similar to a personal initiation, involving an experience of Purgatory where the soul is tested by demonic forces. To prepare for this, secret societies helped foster a sense of individual identity and an "inner narrative". The great initiate-novelists of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, influenced by reformers like John Comenius, forged the modern sense that the interior world has its own meaningful history. This development introduced the idea that childhood involves a different state of mind from adulthood, a concept reflected in works like Jane Eyre and David Copperfield.

The Deeper Laws of Experience

Subjective experience is governed by "deeper laws" that slip between the cracks of conventional physics and psychology. While science views the world objectively, these laws explain the structure of experience and the phenomenon of meaning. These laws include principles such as: challenges ducked will return in new forms; we draw towards us what we fear most; immoral paths eventually demand a price; and to hold onto what you love, you must let it go. These patterns are depicted in the greatest works of literature—such as Oedipus Rex, Hamlet, and War and Peace—because they resonate with the actual shape of human lives.

Practical Applications and Warnings

The Darkening of the Soul

The late nineteenth century saw a spiritual crisis characterized by a "cheating spirit" and an intrusion of demonic forces into the human psyche. Emanuel Swedenborg provided detailed accounts of different hells fashioned by the human capacity for evil, which profoundly influenced the dark realism of Dostoyevsky. Characters in Crime and Punishment and The Brothers Karamazov reflect Swedenborg's visions, suggesting that eternity might be a grimy room filled with spiders rather than a vast celestial expanse.

The Materialization of the Occult

During this era, many esoteric ideas were "materialized" into scientific or psychological theories. Freud’s model of the id, ego, and super-ego is a secularized version of the tripartite cabalistic model, while Jung identified archetypes with the seven major planetary deities. Despite their scientific framing, both men were haunted by occult phenomena; Freud was plagued by "the uncanny" and meaningful coincidences, while Jung eventually concluded that psychological complexes could be independent of the human brain.

Modernism and the Red Initiation

The roots of Modernism are steeped in the occult, with artists like Picasso and Yves Klein exploring subterranean forces and the transfiguration of matter. In the Soviet Union, a darker form of magic emerged known as "the red initiation," where secret agents were trained in former monasteries to exploit the psychic energies of torture and sacrificial victims. In the secret history, Lenin is viewed as a reincarnation of a high priest from a pyramid culture, born again to oppose spiritual evolution.

Gandhi and Soul Force

Gandhi represents the evolution of a new form of consciousness characterized by individual free will and empathy. He utilized spiritual disciplines, such as renouncing sex and using a hand-spun spinning wheel, to work on his "vegetable body" and develop "soul force". His philosophy of Satyagraha taught that acting in accordance with the laws of truth and love would yield a supernatural influence over opponents. He demonstrated that a spirit of self-sacrifice must embrace the whole world rather than just one's own community.
